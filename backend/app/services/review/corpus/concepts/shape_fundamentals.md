title: Shape Fundamentals
tags: [shape, tesuji, efficiency]
---
Good shape is a pattern of stones that gets maximum liberties and flexibility from minimum moves. It matters because Go is a game of efficiency: the side whose stones are working harder usually wins the fight. You don't need to memorize every shape — you need to recognize good shape and reach for it when there's a choice.

Four shapes to know. The tiger's mouth (three stones around one empty point, like a bent triangle) makes an instant eye and is strong; extend or connect into a tiger's mouth when you can. The bamboo joint (two pairs of stones side by side) is uncuttable and very solid — useful when connecting two groups matters more than speed. The table shape (four stones in a square with one corner missing) has six liberties and is a common tesuji for making thickness. Avoid the empty triangle (three stones in an L with no enemy stone filling the diagonal) — it has fewer liberties than the alternatives and is the archetypal bad shape.

A good habit: before playing a contact or forcing move, ask "what shape do I leave behind?" If the resulting shape is an empty triangle or a dumpling (a clumped mass with few liberties), there is almost always a better move one point away.
