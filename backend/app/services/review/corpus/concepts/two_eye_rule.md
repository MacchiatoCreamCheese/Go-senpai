---
id: two_eye_rule
title: Two-Eye Rule
tags: [life_and_death, fundamentals]
---
A group is alive if it has two separate eyes — two internal empty points that the opponent cannot fill without suiciding. This is the most fundamental rule of life and death in Go: one eye is not enough, and bigger eye shapes (three-in-a-row, bent three, square four, pyramid four) all reduce to a question of whether the defender can make two separate eyes.

When reading a life-and-death problem, the first question is always "can this group reach two eyes?" — and the second is "if not, what is the vital point that denies the second eye?" KataGo's largest life-and-death score swings almost always trace back to missing the vital point of an eye-shape.
