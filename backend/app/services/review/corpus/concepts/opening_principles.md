title: Opening Principles
tags: [opening, fuseki, strategy]
---
In the opening (fuseki), the board is mostly empty and every move stakes out a future framework. Three rules of thumb cover most beginner mistakes. First, play the corners before the sides and the sides before the center: corners make territory with the fewest stones because two edges do half the work for you. Second, make an extension from each group you play; a lone stone on the third or fourth line is easy to attack. Third, don't answer every enemy move locally — if your opponent takes a big point, take the next biggest point somewhere else rather than crawling along the edge in response.

A common beginner error is playing contact moves (attaching directly to an enemy stone) in the opening: contact strengthens your opponent. Keep a one- or two-space gap so each stone works efficiently. "Corners, then sides, then center" is not a law, but if your opening feels lost, asking which of the three you skipped usually finds the mistake.
