title: Hane at the Head of Three Stones
tags: [shape, tesuji, proverb]
---
The proverb "hane at the head of three stones" extends the two-stone version: against a three-stone wall the hane is even more severe, because the wall is now committed to a direction and cannot easily turn. The hane becomes a key point that both sides want.

Because the stakes are higher, the defender often has to answer with a counter-hane or a sacrifice. If you played a quiet extension instead of the hane here, KataGo will typically score the mistake at several points — the hane is often a double-purpose move that attacks and takes profit at the same time.
