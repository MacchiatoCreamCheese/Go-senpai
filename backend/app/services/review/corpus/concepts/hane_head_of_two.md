id: hane_head_of_two
title: Hane at the Head of Two Stones
tags: [shape, tesuji, proverb]
---
The proverb "hane at the head of two stones" says that when the opponent has two stones in a row, playing a hane around their leading end is almost always strong. The hane robs the two stones of an extension, threatens to surround them, and often forces the opponent into bad shape if they cut.

This is one of the most reliable shape moves in the game. When KataGo's top move is a hane against a two-stone wall and you played elsewhere, the usual reason for the score drop is that you missed the sente and let the opponent escape forward with no resistance.
