title: Endgame Tesuji
tags: [endgame, yose, tesuji, counting]
---
The endgame (yose) is where many games are decided, not by spectacular captures but by whose last ten moves gained two points more than the opponent's. A tesuji is a clever local move; an endgame tesuji is a clever local move that gains points in the yose.

Three patterns are worth memorizing. The monkey jump is a knight's move from the second line into the opponent's territory along the first line; it reduces a large endgame and is very hard to cut off. The first-line hane-and-connect sequence reduces the opponent's territory by two points at almost no cost and should usually be played in sente. The placement (a stone inside the opponent's framework that threatens to live or reduce) can steal several points if the opponent doesn't respond precisely.

The other half of endgame is timing: play sente moves (moves your opponent must answer) first, reverse-sente second, gote (moves they can ignore) last. Counting the value of each endgame option — rather than playing the first move that looks obvious — is the habit that separates a kyu player from a dan player.
